# PHQ-9 and GAD-7

PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are validated, self-administered tools that detect significant changes in depression and anxiety severity, with reliable improvement defined as reductions of >= 6 points (PHQ-9) and >= 4 points (GAD-7). They show high sensitivity to treatment response over time, though effects may decrease at 6–12 month follow-ups.

## Key Aspects of PHQ-9 and GAD-7 Changes:

- Reliable Improvement/Recovery: Studies show nearly 50% of patients with high initial scores move below the threshold for caseness by the final session. Around 63–70% of patients achieve "reliable improvement".
- Measurement Thresholds:
  - PHQ-9 (Depression): Scores range 0–27; a score >= 10 indicates a likely major depressive episode.
  - GAD-7 (Anxiety): Scores range 0–21; a score >= 10 is commonly used for identifying anxiety disorders.
- Sensitivity to Change: Both tools are highly sensitive to treatment-induced changes, making them ideal for monitoring progress.
- Long-Term Follow-up: While improvement is often maintained, data suggests that average impacts may decline over 6 to 12 months.
- Limitations: The GAD-7 can have a "ceiling effect," where high scores may not accurately reflect further increases in severity.
- Adaptations: Validated adaptations, including British Sign Language (BSL) versions, have adjusted wording (e.g., "Several days" to "A few days") without altering the scoring or psychometric validity.
