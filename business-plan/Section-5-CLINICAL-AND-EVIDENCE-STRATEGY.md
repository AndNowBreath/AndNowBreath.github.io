#### 5.1.3 Phase 2: Evidence Generation - Pilot Study (Months 6-18)

**Study Design:** Prospective cohort study with pre-post outcome measurement

**Research Question:**
Does And Now Breathe (externalized, AI-personalized meditation) reduce anxiety and depression symptoms in adults in addiction recovery, and is it acceptable and engaging for this population?

**Study Type:**

- Non-randomized pilot study (all participants receive intervention)
- Mixed methods (quantitative outcomes + qualitative feedback)
- Pragmatic design (real-world usage, not controlled lab conditions)

**Sample Size:**

- **Target:** n=150 (accounting for 30% dropout, aiming for n=105 completers)
- **Rationale:**
  - Sufficient for detecting medium effect size (d=0.5) with 80% power
  - Larger than typical pilot (n=30-50) to strengthen NHS Apps Library application
  - Feasible within budget and timeline (recovery network can recruit)

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Adults (18-65 years old)
- Currently in addiction recovery (minimum 4 weeks abstinent from primary substance)
- Receiving treatment at partner recovery service OR in self-managed recovery
- Experiencing mild-moderate anxiety or depression (GAD-7 ≥5 or PHQ-9 ≥5)
- Own smartphone or tablet (iOS 12+ or Android 9+)
- Able to read English (future studies will test other languages)
- Willing to use app 3+ times per week for 8 weeks

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Severe mental health crisis (active suicidal ideation, psychosis, mania)
- Unable to provide informed consent
- Currently in residential treatment (unstable living situation affects adherence)
- No access to device or internet

**Recruitment:**

- **Primary:** Partner recovery network (counselors introduce study to eligible clients)
- **Secondary:** Social media ads in recovery communities (Facebook groups, Reddit r/stopdrinking)
- **Tertiary:** NHS addiction services we're piloting with
- **Timeline:** 3 months rolling recruitment (Month 6-9)

**Intervention:**

- **Duration:** 8 weeks
- **Recommended frequency:** 3-5 sessions per week (user-determined, not mandated)
- **App features:** Full access to all techniques (leaves, balloons, lanterns, birds), AI personalization, outcome tracking
- **Support:** Weekly check-in emails, technical support available, optional mid-point check-in call

**Primary Outcomes:**

**1. GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale)**

- **What it measures:** Anxiety symptoms over past 2 weeks
- **Score range:** 0-21 (higher = more anxiety)
- **Clinical significance:** ≥5 = mild, ≥10 = moderate, ≥15 = severe
- **Timepoints:** Baseline, Week 4, Week 8
- **Primary endpoint:** Change from baseline to Week 8
- **Target:** Mean reduction of 3+ points (clinically meaningful)

**2. PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire - Depression)**

- **What it measures:** Depression symptoms over past 2 weeks
- **Score range:** 0-27 (higher = more depression)
- **Clinical significance:** ≥5 = mild, ≥10 = moderate, ≥15 = moderately severe, ≥20 = severe
- **Timepoints:** Baseline, Week 4, Week 8
- **Primary endpoint:** Change from baseline to Week 8
- **Target:** Mean reduction of 4+ points (clinically meaningful)

**Secondary Outcomes:**

**3. Engagement Metrics**

- Sessions completed (total count)
- Average sessions per week
- Session completion rate (% finished vs. abandoned mid-session)
- Techniques used (leaves, balloons, lanterns, birds - frequency of each)
- Retention (% still using app at Week 4, Week 8)

**4. User Satisfaction**

- System Usability Scale (SUS) - 10-item questionnaire, industry standard
- Custom satisfaction questions: "How helpful has this app been?" (1-5 scale)
- Net Promoter Score: "How likely are you to recommend?" (0-10 scale)

**5. Recovery Capital (Addiction-Specific)**

- Brief Assessment of Recovery Capital (BARC-10)
- **What it measures:** Resources supporting recovery (social support, meaningful activities, housing stability, sobriety support)
- **Hypothesis:** App increases recovery capital by providing coping tool, reducing anxiety (which threatens recovery)

**6. Qualitative Feedback**

- Exit interviews with 20 participants (stratified: high engagers, low engagers, dropouts)
- Open-ended questions:
  - "What was most helpful about this app?"
  - "What barriers did you experience?"
  - "How did this compare to other meditation apps you've tried?"
  - "Would you continue using this after the study? Why or why not?"

**Data Collection Procedures:**

**Baseline (Week 0):**

- Online consent form (e-consent, GDPR-compliant)
- Demographics questionnaire (age, gender, substance use history, time in recovery)
- GAD-7, PHQ-9, BARC-10 (via app or online survey)
- Participant receives app access code

**Week 4 (Mid-Point):**

- GAD-7, PHQ-9 (in-app prompt)
- Brief check-in: "How's it going? Any technical issues?" (optional response)

**Week 8 (End-Point):**

- GAD-7, PHQ-9, BARC-10
- SUS, satisfaction questions, NPS
- Invitation for exit interview (compensated £20 for 30-minute interview)

**Follow-Up (Week 16, Optional):**

- GAD-7, PHQ-9only (assess sustainability of effects)
- "Are you still using the app?" (retention beyond study)

**Data Analysis Plan:**

**Quantitative:**

- **Primary analysis:** Paired t-tests (baseline vs. Week 8) for GAD-7 and PHQ-9
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d (small=0.2, medium=0.5, large=0.8)
- **Clinical significance:** % achieving reliable change index (RCI) and clinically significant change
- **Responder analysis:** % achieving ≥50% symptom reduction (GAD-7 or PHQ-9)
- **Engagement analysis:** Correlation between number of sessions and outcome improvement (dose-response)
- **Subgroup analysis:** Outcomes by substance type (alcohol vs. opiates vs. other), time in recovery (<6 months vs. 6-12 months vs. >12 months)

**Qualitative:**

- **Thematic analysis:** Code interview transcripts for recurring themes
- **Categories:** Barriers to use, facilitators of engagement, user experience, therapeutic mechanisms perceived
- **Triangulation:** Compare quantitative engagement data with qualitative reports (do high engagers describe different experiences than low engagers?)

**Ethics & Governance:**

**Ethics Approval:**

- University of Nottingham or Nottingham Trent University Research Ethics Committee
- Application submitted Month 4, approval Month 5 (typical 4-6 week review)
- Annual ethics report (if study extends beyond 12 months)

**Data Protection:**

- GDPR-compliant consent (clear, specific, freely given)
- Data minimization (collect only necessary data)
- Pseudonymization (participant IDs, not names, in analysis dataset)
- Secure storage (encrypted database, access-controlled)
- Data retention policy (5 years post-publication, then deleted)

**Safety Monitoring:**

- Weekly review of crisis keyword triggers (if any participant triggers crisis protocol, research team notified)
- Adverse event reporting (if participant reports worsening symptoms, immediate referral to clinical support)
- Data Safety Monitoring Board (optional for pilot, required for RCT): 1 independent clinician reviews data at Week 4 for any safety concerns

**Expected Outcomes & Implications:**

**Scenario 1: Positive Results (Most Likely)**

- **Findings:** Significant reduction in GAD-7 (mean -3.5 points, p<0.01) and PHQ-9 (mean -4.2 points, p<0.01)
- **Effect size:** Medium (d=0.5-0.6)
- **Engagement:** 65% complete ≥12 sessions (vs. industry standard 15-20% for meditation apps)
- **Satisfaction:** 78% rate 4-5 stars
- **Implications:**
  - ✅ NHS Apps Library Tier 2 application strengthened (evidence of effectiveness)
  - ✅ Sales materials: "Pilot study showed significant anxiety and depression reduction"
  - ✅ Foundation for RCT (larger study to confirm findings)
  - ✅ First peer-reviewed publication (credibility in clinical community)

**Scenario 2: Mixed Results**

- **Findings:** Significant reduction in anxiety (GAD-7) but not depression (PHQ-9), OR significant for high engagers (≥15 sessions) but not overall sample
- **Implications:**
  - ✅ Can still claim anxiety benefit (primary indication)
  - ✅ NHS Apps Library still achievable (Tier 2 requires "acceptable" evidence, not perfect)
  - ⚠️ Refine intervention (why didn't it work for depression? Need more sessions? Different techniques?)
  - → Iterate and run second pilot or proceed to RCT with anxiety as primary outcome

**Scenario 3: Null Results (Low Probability)**

- **Findings:** No significant change in GAD-7 or PHQ-9, low engagement (<30% complete ≥12 sessions)
- **Implications:**
  - ❌ NHS Apps Library Tier 2 unlikely (insufficient evidence)
  - ⚠️ Major product pivot required (intervention not effective OR not engaging)
  - → Options:
    1. Investigate why (qualitative interviews reveal barriers, fix them, re-pilot)
    2. Pivot to general wellness (no therapeutic claims, focus on engagement/experience)
    3. Partner with therapists for facilitated use (not self-directed)
  - → This is low probability because techniques are evidence-based (ACT, mindfulness proven effective), and beta testing should reveal major engagement issues before pilot

**Risk Mitigation:**

- **Beta testing first** (30 users, Months 4-6) catches major UX/engagement problems before expensive pilot
- **Mid-point review** (Week 4 data) allows early intervention if engagement low ("Are people using it? If not, why?")
- **Multiple outcomes** (anxiety, depression, recovery capital, engagement) increases chance that SOMETHING is positive
- **Publication regardless** (null results are publishable, demonstrate transparency, contribute to field knowledge)

#### 5.1.5 Phase 4: Randomized Controlled Trial (Years 3-4)

**Purpose:** Generate gold-standard evidence to support:

1. NHS Apps Library Tier 3 (best practice evidence)
2. NICE guidance consideration
3. Optional: MHRA medical device application
4. Premium pricing justification (evidence = value)
5. International expansion (evidence transfers across markets)

**Study Design:** Superiority RCT (And Now Breathe vs. waitlist control)

**Research Question:**
Is And Now Breathe more effective than waitlist control for reducing anxiety and depression symptoms in adults in addiction recovery?

**Sample Size:**

- **Target:** n=300 (150 intervention, 150 control)
- **Rationale:**
  - Powered to detect small-medium effect size (d=0.4) with 80% power, alpha=0.05
  - Accounting for 25% attrition (dropout)
  - Larger sample = more robust findings, publishable in higher-impact journals
  - Sufficient for subgroup analyses (substance type, severity, etc.)

**Randomization:**

- **Method:** Computer-generated random allocation (1:1 ratio)
- **Stratification:** By baseline anxiety severity (GAD-7 <10 vs. ≥10) to ensure balance
- **Concealment:** Allocation concealed from recruiters until participant enrolled
- **Blinding:**
  - Participants: Cannot be blinded (they know if they get app or waitlist)
  - Assessors: Can be blinded for follow-up interviews (don't know group assignment)
  - Analysts: Blinded until primary analysis complete (dataset labeled Group A vs. Group B)

**Groups:**

**Intervention Group (n=150):**

- Receives And Now Breathe app immediately upon enrollment
- 12-week access (longer than pilot to assess sustained use)
- Recommended 3-5 sessions/week (self-directed frequency)
- All features available (leaves, balloons, lanterns, birds, AI personalization)
- Weekly check-in emails (support, encouragement)
- Technical support available

**Waitlist Control Group (n=150):**

- **No app access for first 12 weeks** (waitlist period)
- Continues usual care (recovery services, therapy, medication if prescribed)
- Receives And Now Breathe app after Week 12 assessments complete (ethical obligation to provide intervention after study)
- **Rationale for waitlist vs. active control:**
  - Waitlist = standard for DTx RCTs (easier recruitment - everyone gets app eventually)
  - Active control (e.g., Headspace) would require licensing costs, more complex comparison
  - Future study could compare to active control, but waitlist sufficient for Tier 3 evidence

**Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria:**

- Same as pilot study (see 5.1.3)
- Added: Minimum 8 weeks abstinent (longer than pilot's 4 weeks, for more stable recovery)

**Recruitment:**

- Multi-site (10-15 NHS addiction services + 5-10 private rehabs)
- Social media advertising (targeted ads in recovery communities)
- GP referrals (via social prescribing link workers)
- Timeline: 6 months rolling recruitment (Year 3, Months 1-6)

**Outcomes:**

**Primary Outcome:**

- **GAD-7 change from baseline to Week 12**
  - Hypothesis: Intervention group will show greater reduction than control
  - Target: Between-group difference of 2+ points (clinically meaningful)

**Secondary Outcomes:**

- PHQ-9 change (depression)
- Recovery capital (BARC-10)
- Quality of life (EQ-5D-5L - health economics measure)
- Engagement (sessions completed - intervention group only)
- Adverse events (both groups monitored)

**Follow-Up:**

- Week 24 (3 months post-intervention): Sustainability of effects
- Week 52 (1 year): Long-term outcomes (if funding allows)

**Data Collection:**

| Timepoint    | Intervention Group                         | Control Group                      |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| **Baseline** | GAD-7, PHQ-9, BARC-10, EQ-5D, demographics | Same                               |
| **Week 4**   | GAD-7, PHQ-9 (in-app)                      | GAD-7, PHQ-9 (email survey)        |
| **Week 12**  | GAD-7, PHQ-9, BARC-10, EQ-5D, satisfaction | GAD-7, PHQ-9, BARC-10, EQ-5D       |
| **Week 24**  | GAD-7, PHQ-9 (follow-up)                   | GAD-7, PHQ-9 (after receiving app) |

**Data Analysis:**

**Primary Analysis:**

- **Intention-to-treat (ITT):** Analyze all randomized participants (even dropouts)
- **Method:** Mixed-effects linear regression (accounts for repeated measures)
- **Covariates:** Baseline severity, substance type, time in recovery
- **Effect size:** Between-group Cohen's d at Week 12
- **Responder analysis:** % achieving ≥50% GAD-7 reduction (intervention vs. control)

**Secondary Analyses:**

- Per-protocol analysis (only those who completed ≥12 sessions)
- Dose-response (within intervention group, do more sessions = better outcomes?)
- Subgroup analyses: Outcomes by substance, gender, severity, device type
- Mediation analysis: Does anxiety reduction mediate depression improvement?

**Health Economics:**

- Cost per QALY (Quality-Adjusted Life Year) gained
- Cost-effectiveness vs. usual care
- **Target:** <£20,000 per QALY (NICE threshold for cost-effective intervention)

**Qualitative Component:**

- Exit interviews with 30 intervention participants, 10 control participants
- Themes: Mechanisms of change, barriers to engagement, suggestions for improvement

**Ethics:**

- Full Research Ethics Committee approval (more rigorous than pilot)
- Trial registration: ISRCTN registry (International Standard Randomised Controlled Trial Number)
- CONSORT reporting (RCT reporting standards - ensures transparency)

#### 5.1.6 Phase 5: NICE Guidance (Years 4-5, Optional)

**What Is NICE Guidance?**

National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) provides independent, authoritative guidance on health treatments and technologies. NICE recommendation = gold standard endorsement in UK healthcare.

**NICE Pathways Relevant to Us:**

**Option 1: Medical Technologies Guidance (MTG)**

- For medical devices and diagnostics
- Requires: RCT evidence, health economics, implementation guidance
- Timeline: 12-18 months from submission to publication
- Recent example: Sleepio (MTG51, 2022) - digital CBT for insomnia

**Option 2: Digital Health Technologies (DHT)**

- Specifically for apps and digital tools
- Requires: Evidence of effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, real-world implementation data
- Timeline: 9-15 months
- Newer pathway (established 2021), clearer fit for apps than MTG

**Why Pursue NICE Guidance:**

1. **NHS Procurement Accelerator:** NICE recommendation = near-automatic commissioning by many trusts
2. **Pricing Power:** Can justify premium pricing (£75-100/user vs. £50 currently)
3. **Competitive Moat:** Very few digital mental health tools have NICE guidance (high barrier to entry)
4. **International Recognition:** NICE = global gold standard (Canada, Australia, others reference NICE)
5. **Acquisition Value:** Significantly increases company valuation (Sleepio's NICE guidance contributed to Big Health's success)

**Requirements:**

**Evidence:**

- ✅ RCT (completed Year 4)
- ✅ Real-world effectiveness data (NHS B2B2C usage data from 25+ sites)
- ✅ Implementation studies (how is it being used in practice? barriers? facilitators?)

**Health Economics:**

- Cost per QALY analysis
- Budget impact assessment (if all eligible patients used it, what's cost to NHS?)
- Cost-effectiveness vs. alternatives (vs. group therapy, vs. no intervention)

**Implementation Guidance:**

- How should NHS services use this? (self-directed vs. therapist-guided)
- Which populations? (all addiction recovery? specific substances? severity levels?)
- Integration with existing care (complement to counseling? Step 2 IAPT? social prescribing?)

**Process:**

**Year 4, Month 6:**

- NICE scoping consultation (submit topic proposal: "And Now Breathe for anxiety in addiction recovery")
- NICE decides whether to progress to full assessment

**Year 4, Month 12:**

- Full submission (200+ page dossier: all evidence, health economics, implementation data)
- External Assessment Group (EAG) appointed (independent academics review our evidence)

**Year 5, Month 6:**

- Committee meetings (expert panel reviews EAG report + our response)
- Public consultation (stakeholders comment on draft guidance)

**Year 5, Month 9-12:**

- Final guidance published
- **Possible outcomes:**
  - **Recommended:** "Evidence supports use of And Now Breathe for anxiety in adults in addiction recovery"
  - **Conditional recommendation:** "Consider And Now Breathe... [with caveats]"
  - **Only in research:** "Insufficient evidence, use only in research studies"
  - **Not recommended:** "Evidence does not support routine use" (unlikely if we have strong RCT)

**Alternative (Faster, Cheaper):**
Instead of NICE, pursue **NHS England commissioning pathway** (national contract for specific indications). Less prestigious than NICE but achieves similar market access, lower cost.

#### 5.1.7 Phase 6: MHRA Medical Device (Year 4+, Optional)

**What Is MHRA?**

Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency - UK regulator for medical devices. Grants medical device classification (Class I, IIa, IIb, III based on risk).

**Why Pursue Medical Device Status:**

1. **Therapeutic Claims:** Can make medical claims ("treats anxiety disorder") vs. wellness claims only ("may help reduce stress")
2. **Reimbursement:** Potential for NHS tariff (payment per patient) vs. service-level contracts only
3. **Insurance Coverage:** Private health insurance may cover (user doesn't pay out-of-pocket)
4. **International Markets:** CE mark (Europe), FDA reciprocity (US may expedite approval for MHRA-approved devices)
5. **Premium Pricing:** Medical devices command higher prices than wellness apps

**Likely Classification:**

**Class I or IIa** (low-medium risk)

- Rationale: Software delivering psychological intervention (not invasive, not high-risk)
- Comparables: Sleepio (Class I), Daylight (FDA Class II, similar to UK IIa)

**Class IIa Requirements:**

**Technical Documentation:**

- Design and development (architecture, user requirements, risk management)
- Clinical evaluation report (summary of all evidence: pilot, RCT, real-world data)
- Usability testing (human factors engineering, user validation)
- Software validation (testing protocols, bug tracking, version control)
- Post-market surveillance plan (how we'll monitor safety and performance)

**Quality Management System (QMS):**

- ISO 13485 certification (quality management for medical devices)
- Design controls, document management, supplier management, complaint handling, corrective actions
- Requires: Staff training, SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures), internal audits

**Notified Body:**

- Third-party organization that assesses medical devices for MHRA
- Reviews technical documentation, QMS
- Conducts audits (initial + annual surveillance)
- Issues CE mark if approved

**Clinical Evidence:**

- RCT results (completed Year 4)
- Real-world safety data (no adverse events in 10,000+ users)
- Literature review (similar interventions, safety profile of meditation-based apps)

**Process:**

**Year 4:**

- Establish QMS (implement ISO 13485 processes)
- Compile technical documentation
- Engage Notified Body (BSI, TÜV SÜD, or similar)

**Year 5:**

- Notified Body review (6-12 months)
- Address any findings (non-conformances, requests for additional data)
- CE mark granted
- Register with MHRA (UK medical device registration)

**Budget:**

| Item                        | Cost        |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ISO 13485 consultant**    | £25,000     | Implement QMS, internal audits, certification support          |
| **Technical documentation** | £30,000     | Regulatory writers prepare clinical evaluation, design dossier |
| **Notified Body fees**      | £15,000     | Application, review, audit                                     |
| **Annual surveillance**     | £5,000/year | Ongoing Notified Body audits                                   |
| **MHRA registration**       | £1,000      | UK medical device registration fee                             |
| **Total (Year 1)**          | £71,000     |                                                                |
| **Annual ongoing**          | £10,000     | Surveillance + QMS maintenance                                 |

**Strategic Decision:**

Medical device pathway is **expensive and administratively complex**. Pursue only if:

1. **Strong clinical evidence:** RCT shows large effect (d>0.6), low adverse events
2. **Reimbursement opportunity:** Evidence that NHS or insurers will pay premium for medical device vs. wellness tool
3. **International expansion:** Planning US market entry (FDA 510(k) easier with existing CE mark)
4. **Competitive pressure:** If competitor achieves medical device status, may need to match

**Alternative:**

Remain "wellness tool with clinical evidence" (NHS Apps Library Tier 3 via NICE guidance). This achieves most commercial benefits (NHS procurement, credibility, pricing) without medical device regulatory burden.

### 5.3 Clinical Validation Metrics

How we'll measure and report clinical effectiveness.

#### 5.3.1 Primary Clinical Outcomes

**GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale)**

**What it measures:**

- 7 questions about anxiety symptoms over past 2 weeks
- Example items: "Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge", "Not being able to stop or control worrying"
- Score: 0-21 (each item 0-3: not at all, several days, more than half the days, nearly every day)

**Clinical significance:**

- Minimal anxiety: 0-4
- Mild: 5-9
- Moderate: 10-14
- Severe: 15-21

**Reliable Change Index (RCI):**

- Reduction of ≥4 points = statistically reliable change
- **Clinically significant change:** Move from clinical range (≥8) to non-clinical (<8)

**Responder definition:**

- ≥50% reduction in score (e.g., baseline 12 → Week 8: 6 or lower)
- OR score <8 at endpoint (non-clinical range)

**Our targets:**

- **Pilot:** Mean reduction of 3+ points (d=0.4-0.5)
- **RCT:** Mean reduction of 4+ points (d=0.5-0.6), 40% responder rate

---

**PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire - Depression)**

**What it measures:**

- 9 questions about depression symptoms over past 2 weeks
- Example items: "Little interest or pleasure in doing things", "Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless"
- Score: 0-27 (each item 0-3)

**Clinical significance:**

- Minimal: 0-4
- Mild: 5-9
- Moderate: 10-14
- Moderately severe: 15-19
- Severe: 20-27

**Reliable Change Index:**

- Reduction of ≥5 points = reliable change
- **Clinically significant change:** Move from clinical (≥10) to non-clinical (<10)

**Responder definition:**

- ≥50% reduction OR score <10 at endpoint

**Our targets:**

- **Pilot:** Mean reduction of 4+ points (d=0.4-0.5)
- **RCT:** Mean reduction of 5+ points (d=0.5-0.6), 35% responder rate

---

#### 5.3.2 Secondary Clinical Outcomes

**Brief Assessment of Recovery Capital (BARC-10)**

**What it measures:**

- Recovery capital = resources supporting recovery (social support, meaningful activities, coping skills)
- 10 items, score 0-50
- Higher score = greater recovery capital (more resources)

**Example items:**

- "I am happy with my living situation"
- "I have enough income to meet my needs"
- "I am proud of the community I live in"
- "I can cope with strong feelings without using drugs or alcohol"

**Clinical relevance:**

- Recovery capital predicts long-term recovery outcomes (Laudet & White, 2008)
- Meditation app may increase recovery capital by: providing coping skill, reducing anxiety (which threatens sobriety), building hope

**Our target:**

- Mean increase of 5+ points (improvement in recovery resources)

---

**EQ-5D-5L (Quality of Life)**

**What it measures:**

- Health-related quality of life across 5 dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression
- Each dimension rated 1-5 (no problems → extreme problems)
- Converted to single utility score (0 = death, 1 = perfect health)

**Why we measure this:**

- Required for NICE submissions (health economics)
- Allows calculation of QALYs (Quality-Adjusted Life Years)
- Example: 0.1 improvement in utility × 1 year = 0.1 QALY gained

**Our target:**

- Mean improvement of 0.08-0.10 in utility score (clinically meaningful)
- Cost per QALY <£20,000 (NICE threshold for cost-effectiveness)

---

#### 5.3.3 Engagement & Usage Metrics

**Primary Engagement Metrics:**

**1. Activation Rate**

- % of users who complete ≥1 session
- Industry benchmark (meditation apps): 60-70%
- **Our target:** 75-80% (onboarding optimization, B2B2C = counselor encouragement)

**2. Active User Rate**

- % of activated users who complete ≥3 sessions
- Industry benchmark: 15-20%
- **Our target:** 35-40% (interactive 3D + AI personalization + clinical population = higher motivation)

**3. Session Frequency**

- Mean sessions per user per week
- Therapeutic efficacy likely requires 3+ per week (based on mindfulness literature)
- **Our target:** 4-5 sessions/week for active users

**4. Session Completion Rate**

- % of sessions completed (not abandoned mid-session)
- Benchmark: 70-80% (some dropout expected, especially if interrupted or technical issue)
- **Our target:** 80%+ (sessions are short [10-15 min], less abandonment than 30-60 min meditations)

**5. Retention**

- **30-day retention:** % still using after 1 month
  - Industry: 10-15%
  - **Our target:** 40%
- **90-day retention:** % still using after 3 months
  - Industry: 5-8%
  - **Our target:** 25%

**6. Technique Usage Distribution**

- % of sessions using each technique (leaves, balloons, lanterns, birds)
- Expected: Leaves most common (daily management), balloons/lanterns/birds less frequent (deeper work)
- Insight: Are users discovering all techniques? Or stuck on one?

---

**Dose-Response Analysis:**

**Hypothesis:** More engagement = better clinical outcomes

**Analysis:**

- Correlate number of sessions completed with GAD-7/PHQ-9 reduction
- Expected relationship: Positive linear (more sessions = greater reduction) up to ~15-20 sessions, then plateau

**Minimum Effective Dose:**

- How many sessions needed to see clinical benefit?
- Hypothesis: 8-12 sessions over 4-6 weeks (based on mindfulness literature)
- **Implication:** If users complete <8 sessions, may not achieve clinical benefit → focus on increasing engagement to this threshold

### 5.4 Safety Monitoring & Adverse Events

Mental health interventions carry inherent risks. We monitor proactively.

#### 5.4.1 Crisis Detection Protocol

**Keyword Monitoring (Automated):**

User inputs scanned in real-time for crisis language:

**Suicide/Self-Harm:**

- "kill myself", "end it", "suicide", "better off dead", "can't go on", "no point living", "want to die"
- "cut myself", "hurt myself", "self-harm", "punish myself"

**Violence:**

- "kill [someone]", "hurt [someone]", "violence", "attack"

**Relapse Intent:**

- "going to use", "need to drink now", "can't resist", "will relapse"

**When Triggered:**

1. **Immediate pause:** Session stops, screen shows resources
2. **Resource display:**
   - **Emergency:** 999 (UK emergency services)
   - **Samaritans:** 116 123 (24/7 crisis line)
   - **Shout:** Text SHOUT to 85258 (crisis text service)
   - **Drinkline:** 0300 123 1110 (alcohol support)
   - **Frank:** 0300 123 6600 (drugs information)
3. **User options:**
   - "I need help now" → Click-to-call Samaritans (direct integration)
   - "I'm okay, just needed to write this down" → Require confirmation: "Are you sure you're safe?" If yes, allow continuation
   - "Exit session" → Safe, gentle close (no judgment language)
4. **Notification (B2B2C only):**
   - If user has opted in (NHS/corporate user with therapist oversight), alert sent to supervising clinician:
     - "User [ID] triggered crisis protocol at [time]. Keyword category: [suicide ideation]. User was shown resources. Follow up recommended."
   - Clinician can reach out via existing relationship (phone, next session)

**Privacy Balance:**

- We do NOT store the specific text that triggered crisis (only category: "suicide ideation", not "I want to kill myself")
- User can review their own inputs (nothing hidden from them)
- Clinician sees alert, not specific thought (user can choose to share in therapy if desired)

---

#### 5.4.2 Adverse Event Reporting

**What We Track:**

**Serious Adverse Events (SAEs):**

- Death (any cause - must report even if unrelated to app)
- Hospitalization (psychiatric or medical)
- Suicide attempt
- Self-harm requiring medical attention

**Non-Serious Adverse Events:**

- User reports worsening symptoms (via feedback form: "I feel worse after using this app")
- Technical issues causing distress (e.g., VR nausea, app crash during emotional moment)
- Inappropriate content generated by AI (e.g., unhelpful or insensitive response)

**Reporting Mechanism:**

**Users can report:**

- In-app feedback button: "Report a problem" (includes "I'm experiencing side effects" option)
- Email: safety@andnowbreathe.com (monitored daily)
- Phone (for urgent): Support line with trained staff (Year 2+)

**Staff process:**

1. **Triage:** Is this SAE (report within 24 hours) or non-serious (report within 7 days)?
2. **Investigation:** What happened? When? Was it related to app use?
3. **Causality assessment:** Unrelated / Possibly related / Probably related / Definitely related
4. **Action:**
   - User support (immediate help connecting to care)
   - Product fix (if technical issue, prioritize bug fix)
   - Documentation (maintain adverse event log)
5. **Reporting:**
   - NHS customers: Notify within 24 hours if SAE involving their user
   - MHRA (if medical device): SAEs reported via Yellow Card scheme
   - Ethics committee: Annual safety report

**Transparency:**

- Annual safety summary published in CIC report (anonymized): "X serious adverse events reported, all assessed as unrelated to app use. Y non-serious events, Z resulted in product improvements."

**For RCT (Year 3-4):**

- 1 patient representative (person in recovery, brings lived experience)

**Meetings:**
